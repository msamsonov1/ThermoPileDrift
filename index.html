Planetary Geothermal Gradient & Thermoelectric Degradation Mechanics

Planetary heat flow probes hammered into lunar polar permafrost measure minute vertical geothermal gradients ($\sim 1.5\text{ to }3.0\text{ K/m}$). Extracting heat flux requires sub-millikelvin precision ($< 1.0\text{ mK}$). However, prolonged exposure to lunar radiation, cryogenic thermal cycling (30 K to 150 K), and violent vibrational shear during regolith hammering induce irreversible electrical contact degradation.

1. Thermopile Differential Seebeck Conversion

The multi-junction differential thermopile generates an open-circuit voltage $V_{\text{out}}$ proportional to the vertical thermal difference $\Delta T = T_2 - T_1$ along the needle stem across $N$ thermocouple pairs with relative Seebeck coefficient $S_{AB}(T)$:

$$V_{\text{out}} = N \int_{T_1}^{T_2} S_{AB}(T)\, dT \approx N \cdot S_{AB}(\bar{T}) \cdot \Delta T$$

Where $S_{AB}(\bar{T}) = S_A(\bar{T}) - S_B(\bar{T})$. For bismuth telluride ($\text{Bi}_2\text{Te}_3$) thin films at cryogenic lunar temperatures ($40\text{ K}$), $S_{AB} \approx 65\text{ }\mu\text{V/K}$. A $\Delta T$ of $1\text{ mK}$ generates an infinitesimal signal of only $65\text{ nV}$, making the readout exceptionally sensitive to series parasitic voltages and lead resistance imbalances.

Governing Law: Onsager Reciprocal Relations in Non-Equilibrium Thermoelectrics

2. Radiation Displacement & Contact Delamination

The bulk thermocouple leg electrical resistance $R_{\text{meas}}$ comprises the intrinsic cryogenic resistance $R_0(T)$, radiation defect scattering $\Delta R_{\text{rad}}$, and mechanical strain-induced contact resistance $R_c$:

$$R_{\text{meas}}(T, t) = R_0 \left(1 + \alpha (T - T_0) + \beta (T - T_0)^2\right) + \Phi_{\text{rad}}(t)\sigma_d \rho_0 + R_c(\varepsilon_m)$$

$\Phi_{\text{rad}}$ represents galactic cosmic ray (GCR) fluence, creating Frenkel vacancy-interstitial pairs that elevate electron scattering. $R_c(\varepsilon_m)$ is governed by Holm contact mechanics: micro-cracking and work-hardening at sputtered micro-junctions alter contact geometry under needle vibrational shock.

Mechanics: Matthiessen’s Rule & Holm Constriction Resistance Formulation

3. Isothermal Boundary Inversion & Lead Subtraction

During scheduled calibration, micro-Peltier elements clamp the needle isothermal ($\Delta T \to 0$), driving Seebeck EMF to zero while probing the two-wire and four-wire excitation response across known temperature boundaries $T_{\text{ref}}$:

$$\hat{R}_p(t) = R_{\text{meas}}(T_{\text{iso}}, t) - R_{\text{nom}}(T_{\text{iso}}); \quad \Delta T_{\text{corrected}} = \frac{V_{\text{meas}} - I_{\text{bias}}\hat{R}_p}{N \cdot S_{AB}(T_{\text{iso}})}$$

By mapping the measured slope $\left(\frac{\partial R}{\partial T}\right)$ against pristine Callendar-Van Dusen polynomials, the firmware decouples intrinsic temperature shifts from extrinsic contact delamination, isolating $\hat{R}_p$ without requiring mechanical probe extraction.

Algorithm: Extended Kalman State Observer with Parameter Tracking

4. Thin-Film Needle Sensor Architecture

The probe consists of a 15-mm diameter grade-5 titanium penetrator needle with a flexible polyimide (Kapton HN) substrate micro-patterned via magnetron sputtering:

$$k_{\text{eff}} = \frac{d_1 k_{\text{ti}} + d_2 k_{\text{poly}} + d_3 k_{\text{regolith}}}{d_1 + d_2 + d_3} \approx 0.014\text{ W}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$$

Thermal conductivity matching minimizes the thermal shunt artifact along the drill borehole. Gold-flashed platinum diffusion barriers ($50\text{ nm}$) prevent interdiffusion of antimony and copper leads over a 10-year surface operational lifetime.
